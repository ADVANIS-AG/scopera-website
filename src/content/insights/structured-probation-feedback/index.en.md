Probation periods often end with a decision made in the moment. What stood out in week two is forgotten by week twelve. What happened most recently weighs more than it should. The outcome then depends more on timing than on performance.

## How a structured rhythm helps

- **Regular conversations**: An agent holds short conversations at fixed intervals, with the new colleague and with the team.
- **Same questions, comparable answers**: Because the structure stays the same, a development over time becomes visible.
- **A whole picture instead of a snapshot**: At the end there is a summary of the entire period.

## Who decides

The decision about the probation period is made by the manager. The agent collects and structures. It does not evaluate and it does not propose an outcome. The conversations are transparent: the person involved knows what is recorded and sees their own feedback.

## Where to start?

Begin with two points in time, for example after four and after ten weeks. That is enough to notice the difference from memory alone.
